# Intelligent Life in the Universe: Does the Cosmos Think in Networks?

When we search for intelligent life in the cosmos, we almost always search for ourselves. Radio waves, cities, technology. But what if the fundamental form of cosmic intelligence is not the individual sender — but the network? What if the pattern that repeats across fungal mycelium, fish schools, and neural nets on Earth is a universal organizing principle?

The question of intelligent life in the universe is old. The answers science provides today are surprisingly concrete — and point in a direction that fundamentally reshapes our understanding of intelligence.

## The Drake Equation: From Thought Experiment to Calculation

In 1961, astronomer Frank Drake wrote an equation on a blackboard that broke the unimaginable into factors: How many communicating civilizations exist in our galaxy? The equation multiplies seven terms — from star formation rate to the fraction of Earth-like planets to the lifespan of technological civilizations.

For decades, it remained an elegant thought experiment because most terms were unknown. That is changing now.

**Stern & Gerya (2024)** published in *Scientific Reports* a revision incorporating geological and planetary conditions. Their key finding: long-term plate tectonics, oceans, and continents are critical prerequisites for communicating civilizations. An Earth-size planet needs 0.007%–0.027% of its mass as water. Only about 33% of planets have the right chemistry for sufficiently dense tectonic plates.

The result: **between 0.006 and 100,000 communicating civilizations** in our galaxy — with the lower bound being more likely.

A study in *Astrobiology* (December 2024) proposed chemical complexity as an estimation framework. Machine learning classifiers (2025) now provide data-driven estimates for previously speculative Drake parameters.

## JWST: The First Real Biosignatures?

The James Webb Space Telescope has shifted the search for life from theory to measurement. In 2025, a University of Cambridge team detected **dimethyl sulfide (DMS)** and dimethyl disulfide (DMDS) in the atmosphere of exoplanet K2-18b — substances produced **exclusively by biological processes** on Earth, primarily by marine phytoplankton.

The significance: a three-sigma detection. Not proof yet, but the strongest signal ever measured from an exoplanet. A review in *PNAS* (Vol. 122, No. 39, 2025) confirms that detecting biosignature gases via transmission spectroscopy is within JWST's capability — though no single "silver bullet" biomarker exists.

Meanwhile, Dr. Sofia Sheikh of the SETI Institute found that planetary radar emissions are detectable up to **12,000 light-years** away. If we broadcast, we are theoretically visible — and vice versa.

## Convergent Intelligence: Why Networks Emerge Everywhere

One of the most remarkable findings in comparative cognition: **complex brains and high intelligence have evolved independently multiple times on Earth.** Mammals, birds, cephalopods, insects — all developed cognitive abilities based on fundamentally different neural architectures.

A review in *Interface Focus* (Royal Society, 2017, Seed et al.) described this as "law-like necessity": in any complex multicellular world, evolution appears to converge toward intelligence. Gueneau et al. (2016) showed that prefrontal areas in mammals and birds converged into highly similar neural structures despite radically different brain designs.

The implication: **intelligence does not arise by accident. It arises lawfully — and it arises in networks.**

### Mycelium: 1.3 Billion Years of Network Intelligence

Fungal mycelium is not a passive root system. It is an adaptive network that makes decisions without a brain.

Yu et al. (2019, *The ISME Journal*) demonstrated that mycelium retains growth directions — a form of **spatial memory**. Adamatzky (2022, *Royal Society Open Science*) documented electrical signal clusters resembling a rudimentary "vocabulary" of up to 50 words. In a single fungal colony, Boolean logic gates can be implemented (*Logica Universalis*, 2022).

### Octopus: Distributed Intelligence in Real Time

The octopus possesses approximately 500 million neurons — two-thirds located in the arms, not the brain. Each arm can independently taste, touch, and make basic motor decisions. Severed arms continue responding to stimuli for up to an hour (Sumbre et al., 2001, *Nature*).

Jennifer Mather (2019, *Animal Sentience*) documented flexibility, causal reasoning, anticipation, and imagination in octopuses — cognitive operations previously attributed only to mammals and birds.

### Swarms: Order from Simple Rules

Tan et al. (2023, *National Science Review*) documented how simple interaction rules between individuals produce emergent intelligent properties: self-organization, robustness, adaptability. The pattern is always the same: **no single actor controls the system. Intelligence emerges from connection.**

## The Fermi Paradox: Why We Don't See Them (Yet)

If intelligence arises so lawfully — where is everyone? Fermi's famous question has received new answers.

The **Mundanity Hypothesis (2025)** proposes that technological development has limits: extraterrestrial civilizations may be advanced but "mundane" — not employing unknown physics, remaining electromagnetically quiet.

A paper in the *International Journal of Astrobiology* (2024) argues the paradox dissolves when we abandon **human-centric assumptions** about what advanced civilizations would do.

On the detection side, Breakthrough Listen achieved a **600x speed improvement** in signal detection in 2025, with the ability to identify entirely new signal types that were previously invisible to searches.

## What This Means

Convergence research reveals something that extends beyond astrobiology: intelligence is not an accident. It is an attractor — a state that complex systems tend toward under the right conditions. And it manifests not as an isolated spark in a single brain, but as an emergent phenomenon in **connected systems**.

Mycelium distributing nutrients through forests. Bee swarms making decisions without any single bee having the full picture. Neurons thinking in networks, not as individual cells.

When we search for intelligent life in the cosmos, we may be looking for the wrong form. Not the lone transmitter on a single planet — but the network that emerges whenever complexity crosses a certain threshold.

Physics cannot prove this yet. But biology on Earth suggests: **wherever conditions are right, intelligence arises. And it arises in networks.**

## FAQ

**How many intelligent civilizations exist in our galaxy?**
Current Drake equation revisions (Stern & Gerya, 2024) estimate between 0.006 and 100,000 communicating civilizations, with the lower bound more likely. The exact number depends on geological and chemical prerequisites only recently becoming quantifiable.

**What did JWST find on K2-18b?**
Dimethyl sulfide (DMS) and dimethyl disulfide (DMDS) — substances produced exclusively by living organisms on Earth. The detection is at three sigma: promising but not yet proof. Further observations are planned.

**What is convergent intelligence?**
The independent emergence of similar cognitive abilities in unrelated species. Mammals, birds, octopuses, and even insects have developed problem-solving, memory, and tool use based on entirely different brain architectures — suggesting a universal principle.

**Can fungi really think?**
Not in the human sense. But mycelial networks demonstrably exhibit spatial memory, electrical signal patterns resembling vocabulary, and the ability to implement Boolean logic gates. It is a substrate-independent form of information processing.

**Why haven't we received a signal from extraterrestrials?**
Possibly because advanced civilizations are electromagnetically quiet, because we searched for the wrong signal types (SETI only gained new-morphology detection in 2025), or because distances are so vast that signals haven't reached us yet. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
